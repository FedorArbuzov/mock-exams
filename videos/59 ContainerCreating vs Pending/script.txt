Your Pod is not Running yet - is that already a failure?

Usually no. Pending and ContainerCreating are two different early stages, and both can be perfectly normal for a few seconds. The difference tells you exactly where the Pod is in its journey.

Pending means the scheduler has not placed the Pod on a node yet, or it is placed but not ready to start. Common causes: no node has enough CPU or memory, taints block it, or a PersistentVolumeClaim is unbound. ContainerCreating means the Pod already has a node and the kubelet is now pulling the image, mounting volumes, and setting up networking.

What beginners get wrong: deleting Pods that are simply Pending for resources, which changes nothing. Or assuming ContainerCreating is stuck when it is just pulling a large image.

Practical check: kubectl describe pod and read Events. Pending points at scheduling - nodes, taints, PVC. ContainerCreating stuck points at image pull, volume mounts, or CNI networking.

Rule to remember: Pending is a scheduling problem, ContainerCreating is a startup problem.
