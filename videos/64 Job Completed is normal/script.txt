Your Job shows Completed and the Pod is gone - did it break?

No - for a Job, Completed is success. Unlike a Deployment, a Job is meant to run to the end and then stop. Its Pod finishing with exit code zero is exactly the goal. A Deployment Pod that exits would restart forever; a Job Pod that exits cleanly is done on purpose.

This trips people up because the mental model from long-running apps says a stopped Pod is bad. For batch work - a migration, a backup, a one-time import - stopping is the whole point.

What beginners get wrong: panicking that the Pod disappeared, or setting a restartPolicy of Always on a Job, which is invalid - Jobs use OnFailure or Never. Also confusing Completed with Failed - Failed means the Pod exhausted its retries with a non-zero exit.

Practical check: kubectl get jobs shows COMPLETIONS like one of one. kubectl describe job shows Succeeded. To read output after the Pod is cleaned up, grab logs before ttlSecondsAfterFinished removes it, or ship logs to a collector.

Rule to remember: for a Job, Completed means it worked - only Failed is a problem.
