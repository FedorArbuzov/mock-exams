The container crashed and kubectl logs shows nothing - now what?

Use dash dash previous. When a container restarts, the current logs belong to the fresh instance, which may have just started or be empty. The evidence of the crash lives in the previous container's logs. kubectl logs your-pod dash dash previous prints them.

Know the other flags too. If a Pod has multiple containers, add dash c and the container name, or logs will complain it does not know which one. dash f follows live output. dash dash tail equals 100 limits volume. dash dash since equals 10m scopes to a time window.

What beginners get wrong: reading empty current logs during a CrashLoopBackOff and concluding there is nothing to see. Or forgetting dash c on a multi-container Pod and getting an error instead of output.

Practical flow: kubectl get pods shows rising RESTARTS, then kubectl logs dash dash previous reveals the stack trace or the config error that killed it.

Rule to remember: crash loop means read dash dash previous, not the empty current log.
