A Pod won't start - where do you look first?

kubectl describe. Where get shows status, describe shows the story. It prints the object's full configuration and, crucially, the Events at the bottom - the timeline of what the scheduler and kubelet actually tried and why it failed.

Those Events are gold. FailedScheduling means no node fit - resources or taints. Failed with ImagePullBackOff means the image could not be pulled. Unhealthy means a probe is failing. Back-off restarting means a crash loop. You read the reason instead of guessing.

What beginners get wrong: jumping straight to logs when the container never even started, so logs are empty. describe would have shown the real blocker - a missing ConfigMap, an unschedulable Pod, or a failing readiness probe.

Practical flow: kubectl describe pod your-pod, scroll to Events, read the most recent lines first. Then, only if the container did start, move to logs.

Rule to remember: describe first, read the Events, then dig deeper.
