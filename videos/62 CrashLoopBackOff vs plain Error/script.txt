Why is one failure just Error and another CrashLoopBackOff?

They describe two different moments. Error, or an exit-code state, means the container's current run exited with a failure and it is not being retried right now. CrashLoopBackOff means the container started, crashed, restarted, crashed again - and Kubernetes is now inserting a growing delay between restarts to stop the thrash.

CrashLoop is not a root cause; it is a symptom of repeated crashes. The real reason is in the app - an unhandled exception on boot, a missing env var, a failing dependency, or a liveness probe killing a healthy-but-slow process.

What beginners get wrong: treating CrashLoopBackOff as the error itself and googling it forever, instead of reading why the process exits. Or missing that the backoff delay grows, so restarts get slower, not stuck.

Practical flow: kubectl logs dash dash previous to see the last crash output, kubectl describe pod for the exit code and probe events, then fix the boot error and let restarts settle.

Rule to remember: CrashLoopBackOff is repeated crashing - read logs previous for the real cause.
