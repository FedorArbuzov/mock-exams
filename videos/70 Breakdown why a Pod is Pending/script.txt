Your Pod is stuck in Pending - where do you even start?

Pending almost always means the scheduler cannot place the Pod on any node. Walk a fixed order and you will find it fast.

First, node resources. If every node lacks the CPU or memory your Pod requests, it stays Pending. Lower the requests or add capacity. Second, taints and tolerations. Nodes can carry taints that repel Pods; without a matching toleration, your Pod is refused. Third, an unbound PersistentVolumeClaim. If the Pod needs storage and no PersistentVolume or StorageClass satisfies the claim, scheduling waits. Fourth, affinity and node selectors that are too strict - a nodeSelector or required affinity rule that no node matches.

What beginners get wrong: deleting and recreating the Pod, which changes nothing, or blaming the image when the container never even got scheduled.

Practical flow: kubectl describe pod and read the Events at the bottom - FailedScheduling spells out the reason: Insufficient cpu, node had untolerated taint, or no persistent volumes available. That line tells you which of the four to fix.

Rule to remember: Pending is scheduling - check resources, taints, PVC, affinity, then Events.
