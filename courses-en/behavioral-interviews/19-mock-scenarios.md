# 19. Mock scenarios with a rubric

## Intro

Behavioral without mocks is like algorithms without a timer. The scenarios below are for self-check or a pairing with a colleague. The rubric mirrors an interviewer's.

---

## Rubric (1–4)

| Score | Signal |
|------|--------|
| 1 | no structure, blame, no result |
| 2 | partial STAR, vague metrics |
| 3 | clear STAR, your contribution, a result |
| 4 | + learning, options, scale, follow-on impact |

**Hire bar** for middle: an average of **≥3** across 5 questions.

---

## Scenario A — Hiring manager (45 min)

| # | Question | Story from the bank |
|---|--------|-----------------|
| 1 | Tell me about yourself | pitch |
| 2 | A hard technical trade-off | 10 |
| 3 | Conflict | 06 |
| 4 | Failure | 07 |
| 5 | Why us / why you're leaving | 12 |
| 6 | Your questions | 15 |

---

## Scenario B — Peer (45 min)

| # | Question |
|---|--------|
| 1 | An example of code review |
| 2 | Working with unclear requirements |
| 3 | Mentoring |
| 4 | Deadline |
| 5 | What you look for in a teammate |

---

## Scenario C — Bar raiser lite (60 min)

Deep follow-ups on **one** ownership story:

- What exactly did **you** do?
- Alternatives?
- What went wrong?
- How did you measure it?
- Would you do it again?

---

## Subtasks

**Time:** ~90–120 min (at least one full scenario).

### 19.1 Self-mock A (45 min)

Recording + self-assessment against the rubric.

### 19.2 Partner mock B (45 min)

A colleague scores you; feedback in writing.

### 19.3 Deep dive C (30 min)

15 min on one story — 8 follow-ups.

### 19.4 Gaps (15 min)

A table: question | score | what to improve.
