# 05. Tell me about yourself

## Intro

The first question of almost every round. The goal is a **frame**: who you are, why here, why now. Not an autobiography from childhood, and not reading your resume line by line.

---

## Structure (2–3 min)

```text
1. Present  — who you are now (role, domain, 1 strength)
2. Past     — 2–3 relevant steps (not every job)
3. Future   — why this role/company (a bridge to the position)
```

**Example skeleton (backend):**

"For the last 3 years — backend in fintech, FastAPI and Postgres, focused on payment reliability. Before that — integrations in e-commerce. Now I'm looking for a product with a strong platform culture where I can own a service end-to-end — that's why I applied for your middle backend role."

---

## Length versions

| Version | When |
|--------|-------|
| 30 sec | recruiter, "briefly about yourself" |
| 2 min | standard hiring manager |
| 5 min | only if asked for "more detail" |

Write both; in the interview start with the **short** one and offer to go deeper.

---

## Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|--------|-------------|
| Chronology since 2010 | only the relevant parts |
| "I studied at…" for 2 min | education in one phrase, unless a fresh grad |
| No link to the role | Future → their product |
| Filler words with no structure | rehearse 10 times |

---

## Link to the resume

Every Past phrase must be **backed** by a bullet in your CV — the interviewer cross-checks. A mock-exams capstone is fine: "in parallel I was going deeper into K8s/GitOps on a learning sandbox".

---

## Subtasks

**Time:** ~55 min.

### 5.1 A 2-min draft (20 min)

Write the text; read it out loud with a timer.

### 5.2 A 30-sec version (10 min)

Compress to 4–5 sentences.

### 5.3 Tailored to the role (15 min)

Two Future variants for different company types (product vs outsourcing).

### 5.4 Recording (10 min)

A voice recording; one pass with fewer than 3 "uhhh"s — otherwise re-record.
