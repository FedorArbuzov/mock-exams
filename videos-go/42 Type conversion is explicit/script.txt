Why will Go not cast types silently for me?

Because silent conversions hide bugs until production. In other languages you might add an integer and a float without a second thought. In Go you write int64 of count explicitly, so the compiler forces you to see the conversion.

That stops painful surprises. Casting a large thirty-two-bit integer down to eight bits truncates the high bits - you store three hundred and read back forty-four. Mixing signed and unsigned types without a cast is a compile error, not a quiet wrap at runtime. Converting float64 to int drops the decimal part - three point nine nine becomes three, not four.

The rule is simple - if the types differ, convert explicitly. When the reason is not obvious, add a one-line comment so the next reader knows you meant that truncation.

It feels verbose on day one. It feels like armor on day three hundred.
